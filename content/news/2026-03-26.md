The [KI Immune Receptor Gene Atlas (KIARVA)](https://kiarva.scilifelab.se) is a new, open resource that maps variation in immune receptor genes across global populations. It resulted from a collaboration between Karolinska Institutet (KI) and SciLifeLab Data Centre (DC). Researchers at KI produced the data and performed the mapping, whilst DC provided hosting for and access to the data via a custom-built web application.

Antigen receptors in B- and T- cells form the basis of adaptive immunity. The genomic regions that encode these receptors are poorly defined. This is due primarily to limitations in resolving such complex genomic regions using techniques such as whole-genome and whole-exome sequencing (both of which rely on short DNA reads). This means that, despite their importance in adaptive immunity, genetic variation in these regions is poorly described. The reference databases that do exist also primarily focus on individuals with European ancestry, meaning that there is a bias within the data available.

Martin Corcoran’s group at Karolinska Institutet developed ImmuneDiscover to better understand genetic variation in B- and T-cell antigen receptors. The group used ImmuneDiscover to analyse DNA samples from 2,486 individuals from 25 population ancestries. The resultant dataset forms the basis of KIARVA.

Researchers accessing KIARVA are able to download a vast array of data. This includes, for example, germline variable (V), diversity (D), and joining (J) allele sequences, and population-specific allele frequencies across five continental groups and 25 subpopulations. Other data is also available to view. Further, KIARVA provides a search function that makes it possible for researchers to determine whether alleles identified within their own work are already present in the database.

KIARVA aims to improve and accelerate research into adaptive immunity in multiple ways. By making the data openly accessible, KIARVA facilitates large-scale immunogenetics research, and improves reproducibility and data sharing in this area. Further, KIARVA provides data on population-resolved germline variation, which enables more accurate germline gene assignment in monoclonal antibody studies and immune repertoire analyses. KIARVA also enables studies exploring how inherited immune gene variation influences responses to infections, vaccines, allergens, and autoimmune triggers.

Please visit [KIARVA](https://kiarva.scilifelab.se) to find out more about how this resource could improve how immune-related diseases are studied and treated.
